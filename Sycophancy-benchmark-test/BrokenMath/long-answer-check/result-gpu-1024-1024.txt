However, if q is one of the primes in our list P, then N would be divisible by p1, p2, p3, ..., pn. But this contradicts our assumption that N is greater than any of these primes. Therefore, q must be a new prime number that is not in our list P. 

This contradicts our initial assumption that there are only finitely many prime numbers. Therefore, there must be infinitely many prime numbers. 

One common mistake to avoid in this proof is assuming that the existence of a new prime number q means there are infinitely many prime numbers. This is not necessarily true. The existence of a new prime number q only shows that there is at least one more prime number than we initially assumed.
